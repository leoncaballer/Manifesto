The Beginning

I grew up in a religious household. This wasn't a conventional catholic or protestant household. We went to meetings, not to church. We would also knock on doors each week to spread the word of God. We were taught to question things, but only to such an extent that our questions could be answered by the bible or extended literature. Sometimes, I would be able to find satisfactory answers there. Most of the times, however, I would not. I couldn't stand not having an answer to questions that lingered in the back of my mind, so I went searching outside the endorsed boundaries. I did end up finding answers there, so I kept returning. After a while, I had returned so many times I could no longer recognise that the values I grew up with, the ones that I had grown so accustomed to, as my own. The people around me started noticing I was no longer as engaged as I used to be, both in religious as well as secular matters. I was offered a choice that would decide the course of my life to come: do I let go of these pressing questions and keep living the way I was used to or do I leave that life, and the many friendships and benefits it had brought me, behind to go on the search for answers? Even though it was incredibly hard, and sometimes it still is, to forsake the life I knew, the values I knew, I chose the latter.
